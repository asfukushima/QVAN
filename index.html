QVAN is a nonprofit 501(c)(3) library, publishing studio, and residency serving San Francisco, CA and beyond. Operating as an open-access, non-commercial platform, QVAN seeks to document ephemeral, underrepresented, and community-driven visual practices that exist both within and outside institutional frameworks.

01 — Mission

Why We Exist

QVAN supports artists, activists, designers, and cultural workers by creating a living, evolving archive of queer visual production. The project centers work that engages with resistance, identity formation, and world-building, with a particular focus on practices emerging from marginalized communities within queer ecosystems.

Founded in 2026, QVAN emerged in response to the ongoing erasure, fragmentation, and commodification of queer histories online. The platform prioritizes accessibility, collective authorship, and the preservation of materials that often resist traditional archival categorization.

02 — Aims

What We're Working Toward

QVAN aims to preserve vulnerable and ephemeral digital and physical materials; provide open, equitable access to queer visual histories; support experimental, non-normative modes of archiving and publishing; foster cross-generational dialogue and knowledge exchange; and resist extractive and institutionalized models of cultural preservation.

03 — In Solidarity

Our Political Position

QVAN supports defunding and abolishing the police, the military, ICE, and prisons, and doing the difficult work required to dismantle white supremacy at all scales — from individual to organizational, from community to national.

For QVAN, this means a particularly close examination of whiteness and how it continues to operate within art, design, and educational institutions, especially as one of the core intersecting forces of oppression defined as the matrix of domination. We strive to hold ourselves accountable in all programming and decision-making.

04 — Commitments

What We Commit To

To maintain programming and resources that are always free to use. To maintain a safe space for experimental publishing, free from unnecessary judgment, available for all anti-racists — particularly artists, writers, organizers, students, activists, and educators, and especially those who are LGBTQ+.

To support the work of LGBTQ+ artists, writers, and small presses. To assemble underrepresented review teams for residency and programming decisions. To prioritize access for those most impacted by the matrix of domination: white supremacy, heteropatriarchy, settler colonialism, and capitalism.

Our
Commitments
In Full
To maintain programming and resources that are always free to use.

To maintain our risograph printer and supplies and make them available for free printing for anti-racist art, organizing, and activism.

To assemble underrepresented review teams when determining access to artist’s residencies and other QVAN programming, and to always include local, community-based artist representation on these teams.

To support the work of LGBTQ+ artists, writers, and small presses by acquiring their work for our growing library of experimental publishing, and to maintain free access to the collection for reference, research, and inspiration.

To put these advantages and privileges to work for the benefit of LGBTQ+ artists and publishers.

To learn how to better support our community of LGBTQ+ artists and publishers, and to prioritize access for those who are most underserved and impacted by the matrix of domination, white supremacy, heteropatriarchy, settler colonialism, and capitalism.

Space +
Tools

QVAN exists as a physical archive, library, and production space, where preservation and publishing are collective, embodied practices rather than purely digital ones. The project centers access to materials, tools, and shared space as a means of sustaining queer cultural production offline.

QVAN operates as an active site of making, gathering, and circulation, where visitors engage directly with archival materials and contribute to their ongoing transformation.

QVAN supports in-person contribution and participation, allowing individuals and collectives to bring materials directly into the space for scanning, duplication, annotation, or redistribution. The space is open to all community members, with sliding-scale access and no one turned away for inability to pay.
Section 02 — Access
Community
Access &
Membership

QVAN runs on a sliding-scale model. No one is turned away for inability to pay. All programming is open to the public. Members receive additional studio hours, digitization support, and archival research assistance from QVAN staff.

Open Studio Hours

Tuesday–Saturday, 10am–6pm. Drop-in welcome; booking recommended for risograph and digitization suite.

Monthly Workshops

Zine-making, protest poster design, archival care, screen printing, and bookbinding. All skill levels. Offered monthly.

Residency Program

Semester-long residencies for artists working in print, publication, or archival practice. Applications open annually.

Research Access

Scholars, students, and community researchers may schedule extended sessions with reading room access and support from QVAN staff.
